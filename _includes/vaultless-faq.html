Answers to the most frequently asked questions.

All credentials are shipped to the users wallet which are assigned the necessary credentials.

Let's take the example of an active directory user a@companydomain.com. a@acompanydomain.com is assigned active directory domains - domain1 and domain2. This user will get assigned credentials related to both domains upon onboarding to AuthNull. When the user wants to access a privileged user account say mysql-admin, then when checkedout - mysql-admin credentials are additionally sent to a@companydomain.com's wallet. This could include SSH Keys and / or passwords should that option be enabled as a factor.

If your organization has a credential policy, then all credentials - passwordless credentials, SSH Keys and Passwords are rotated and automatically shipped to respective assigned user's wallets.

Passwordless credentials (also called as Verified credentials - VCs), SSH Keys and Passwords are the kinds of credentials that are stored in the vault.

All credentials issued to users' wallets are automatically revoked when the users are removed from an Identity solution as a part of the Join-Manage-Leave (JML) Lifecycle. Additionally, administrators can force revoke, expire existing wallets as well as credentials.

As a part of the checkout process, previous credentials are expired and new credentials are issued. This means the users who previously held credentials in their vault will see it become inaccessible and the new users will get credential notifications.
